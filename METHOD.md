# Pricing Power: the method

**Win bigger deals at the price you want. Start with the problem, not the price.**
One page. About 20 minutes. Done once per offer, sharpened after every call.

---

## The idea in one line

Buyers don't buy your product. They buy their way out of a problem.
A buyer who has put a number on the problem compares your price to that number, not to your competitors. That's pricing power.
So: know the 3 problems you solve better than anyone, down to the source, and let the buyer name what they cost. You lead the conversation instead of chasing it. 🐬

## The chart

Three problems. Three columns. Nine boxes.

| | 1. PROBLEM (in their words) | 2. IMPACT (what it costs them) | 3. SOURCE (what really causes it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | | | |
| **B** | | | |
| **C** | | | |

- **Problem** = what the client says. Their sentence, not your diagnosis. "I'm still the only one who closes."
- **Impact** = what the problem costs. Money, time, and how it feels. If nobody would pay to make it stop, it isn't a problem, it's a complaint.
- **Source** = the real cause underneath. The thing that, fixed, makes the problem go away. This is where you win, because it's the one thing the buyer usually can't see from inside.

## The 5 steps

**1. Spray (5 min).** Write down every problem your clients have. Aim for 10+. No filtering yet.

**2. Focus (5 min).** Keep 3. A problem only survives if it passes all three filters:
- ✅ **You can fix the source.** Not the symptom. The source. If you can't, cross it out, however real it is.
- ✅ **A real client said it.** In their own words, on a call, in an email, in a review. If you can't quote one, it's a guess. Mark it 🔍 and verify it on your next call.
- ✅ **It costs real money or time.** Big enough that doing nothing hurts.

**3. Burn down (5 min).** Fill the three columns for each problem: Problem → Impact → Source.
Keep asking "and what causes that?" until you hit something you fix. That's the source.

**4. Turn it into questions (5 min).** One question per box. Questions, not pitch lines:
- Problem → *"What's the frustration with [X] right now? What have you tried?"*
- Impact → *"What does that cost you? What happens if nothing changes in the next 6 months?"*
- Source → a question that lets the buyer find the source themselves. *"When a deal goes quiet, what happened in the call before?"*

The buyer says the source out loud. You don't. When they say it, it's theirs, and so is the urgency. 🐯

**5. Test (every call).** After each conversation, mark each problem:
- ✅ the buyer confirmed it in their own words (write the sentence down, it's gold for your copy)
- ❌ it didn't land
Two ❌ in a row and nothing ✅? Replace the problem with the next one from your Spray list.

## How it maps to the 8 Steps of the Repeatable Sale

| Column | Step in the call |
|---|---|
| Problem | Step 2: Frustration |
| Impact | Step 3: Importance + Step 4: Cost of Inaction |
| Source | sets up Step 5: Deliverables (what they want fixed, in their words) |

Pricing Power is your homework. The 8 Steps are the conversation. Homework done, you walk in calm and in control.

## Where it comes from (and what we changed)

Pricing Power is inspired by Keenan's *Gap Selling* and his Problem Identification Chart (Problem, Root Cause, Impact). It's a great book. We kept the core and changed what founders and reviewers struggle with:

| What people say about the original | What Pricing Power does |
|---|---|
| Discovery turns into an interrogation. Buyers say "get to the point." | **Max 3 problems.** Short, sharp, one question per box. |
| "I know your problem better than you" can sound arrogant. | **Every problem needs a real client quote.** No quote = a guess to verify, not a claim. |
| It's hard to stop pitching. | **The output is questions, not a pitch.** The buyer names the source. |
| Lots of problems you can't actually solve end up on the chart. | **Solvability filter.** If you can't fix the source, it's out. |
| Little hard evidence it works. | **✅/❌ after every call.** Your own data, problem by problem. |
| Too heavy for every deal. | **Built once per offer**, sharpened over time. Not a form per deal. |

## Use it for more than calls 🌴

The 3 problems and the client sentences you collect in step 5 are your best headlines, cold-email openers, post hooks and workshop titles. They're already in the buyer's words.
